Caveats for reading the tables:

  • Correctness gating: on claim-office, trophies for quality/efficiency metrics go only to cells at verification_pct 1.00. single-context-v2/o5 (0.79), hybrid-v6/o5 (0.95) and hybrid-v2/o5 (0.99) are exempt from the gate for the quality columns because their shortfall traces to a single acceptance scenario (F-1.4), not to stub implementations — but they carry no correctness trophy.
  • cycle_count, refactorings_applied and predictions_correct_rate are n/a for inline-tdd-v1, not zero. inline-tdd-v1 prescribes no phase markers, so the parser has nothing to count. See MARKERS.md.
  • Code Mass (APP) carries no trophy. It ranks the cells opposite to decomposition (F-1.6) and has no notion of nesting — established in RQ-architecture-axis-sol-pi F-1.9.
  • cycle_count is ambivalent and carries no trophy.
  • † cycle_count and refactorings_applied come from the retired marker parser. The current pipeline does not produce either field, and the phase chain that replaced it wrote nothing for these runs, so both rows are frozen at their last measured values and cannot be recomputed. Read them as provenance, not as current measurements; the comparable successors are cycles_closed and refactor_events.
  • cost_usd is a list-price comparison, not an invoice: these runs went through the native Anthropic API on a subscription plan, where nothing is billed per token. On claim-office the four opus-5 cells read $3.89 / $47.56 / $61.94 / $109.59 along the axis, against $2.92 / $12.72 / $29.28 / $53.77 on opus-4-7.

F-1.1 — H1 confirmed: the architecture ranking holds on opus-5, on both katas

The inline-tdd-v1 → single-context-v2 → hybrid-v2 → hybrid-v6 ordering on avg_loc_per_function is monotone on claim-office and near-monotone on game-of-life, exactly as on opus-4-7.

Kata inline-tdd-v1 single-context-v2 hybrid-v2 hybrid-v6 Monotone?
claim-office / opus-5 9.18 5.89 4.04 3.21 🏆 yes
claim-office / opus-4-7 13.07 10.03 5.75 3.67 🏆 yes
game-of-life / opus-5 6.69 4.12 4.54 3.57 🏆 single-context-v2/hybrid-v2 swap (within σ)
game-of-life / opus-4-7 16.52 9.58 6.56 3.62 🏆 yes

Rationale: the ranking transfers across the model generation. This is the opposite of the Sol result (RQ-architecture-axis-sol-pi F-1.6), where structureless inline-tdd-v1 beat every architecture on game-of-life and hybrid-v2 collapsed to 1.6 functions per implementation. The mechanism proposed there — the isolated refactor subagent not performing its extraction — does not apply to opus-5: hybrid-v6 averages 8.6 refactorings on game-of-life and 43.4 on claim-office.

The single-context-v2/hybrid-v2 swap on game-of-life (4.12 vs 4.54) sits inside one σ (1.00 / 0.92) and is read as "indistinguishable", not as a rank change.

Consequence: the reduction chain built on hybrid-v1 rests on an architecture opus-5 supports. H2 (Sol pattern) and H3 (metric split) are both refuted for this model.

Rationale: on both katas, inline-tdd-v1/opus-5 lands at roughly the cognitive_max of hybrid-v2/opus-4-7 (5.3 vs 5.71; 7.2 vs 6.5) — a ~15-line prompt with no agents, no skills and no phase structure now produces what previously took the full hybrid architecture. Rationale: the quality gain is real and consistent, but its size (0.17–0.53×) is an order of magnitude smaller than the cost (6.8–36×). The intermediate cells are the interesting ones for practice: hybrid-v2 reaches avg_loc_per_function 3.81 on claim-office at 94.2 M tokens — 58 % of hybrid-v6's cost for 89 % of its decomposition gain over inline-tdd-v1.

Token figures here count subagent consumption; the three structured arms all spawn subagents and were recorded short until 2026-09-30 (RQ-old-vs-new-exact-line-opus55 F-4.12.5). The correction widens every factor in this finding rather than narrowing it, because the baseline arm spawns none.

F-1.4 — One acceptance scenario fails across workflows and accounts for most of the correctness spread

Eighteen opus-5 runs on claim-office pass all internal tests but miss external scenarios. Seventeen of the eighteen fail on the same one.

Workflow n at verification_pct < 1 Failing scenario
single-context-v2 2 of 5 14-family-steinheim (1×), total failure (1×)
hybrid-v2 11 of 18 14-family-steinheim
hybrid-v6 5 of 6 14-family-steinheim

Rationale: 14-family-steinheim fails for subagents-v1, single-context-v2, hybrid-v2 and hybrid-v6 alike on opus-5 — it is a kata/model interaction, not a workflow defect. Any correctness comparison between opus-5 cells on claim-office therefore measures partly this one scenario. The hybrid-v6 figure of 0.94 means "14 of 15 scenarios in five runs, 15 of 15 in one", not "systematically less correct". The same holds for hybrid-v2 at 0.96: eleven runs miss that one scenario and seven miss nothing.

The exception is the one single-context-v2 run at verification_pct 0: it stopped after 2 cycles with 6 functions and 60 passing self-written tests. That is the internal/external divergence documented as F-1.2 in RQ-architecture-axis-sol-pi, and it is what drags the single-context-v2 cell mean to 0.79 (σ 0.44) — the cell is bimodal, not uniformly mediocre.

F-1.5 — single-context-v2 is the least reliable architecture on opus-5, hybrid-v2 the most efficient compromise

claim-office verification_pct per run σ cycle_count range †
single-context-v2 0 / 0.93 / 1 ×3 0.44 2–48
hybrid-v2 0.93 ×11 / 1 ×7 0.03 38–52
hybrid-v6 0.93 ×5 / 1 0.03 36–52

Rationale: single-context-v2's shared single context has a failure mode where the model declares the task complete after 2 cycles. hybrid-v2 and hybrid-v6, which isolate the refactor phase, do not show it in 24 runs. This is the same instability the Sol RQ documented for single-context-v2 (F-1.4 there, refactorings_applied σ 17.4) and it reproduces on a different model and harness.

Against that, hybrid-v2 delivers 94.2 M tokens and 44.2 minutes for avg_loc_per_function 3.81 — against hybrid-v6's 161.6 M and 91.9 minutes for 3.19. The marginal decomposition gain from hybrid-v2 to hybrid-v6 (0.62 LoC per function) costs 72 % more tokens and 108 % more wallclock.

F-1.6 — Code Mass (APP) ranks the cells opposite to decomposition, on this model too

Kata metric inline-tdd-v1 single-context-v2 hybrid-v2 hybrid-v6
claim-office avg_loc_per_function (lower = better) 8.90 5.89 3.81 3.19
claim-office Code Mass (APP) 758.2 569.0 859.5 997.0
game-of-life avg_loc_per_function 6.48 4.12 4.23 3.46
game-of-life Code Mass (APP) 196.8 176.2 179.0 195.8

Rationale: APP fails to reward decomposition on both katas, though not identically. On claim-office the inversion is clean — the best-decomposed cell carries the highest Code Mass (997.0 against the structureless arm's 758.2). On game-of-life the two are within a point of each other (195.8 against 196.8), so the ordering is no longer strictly inverted there; what survives is that the best-decomposed cell is still at the top of the range rather than the bottom. APP measures compactness and has no notion of nesting or naming; a workflow that extracts more named functions scores worse on it by construction. This replicates RQ-architecture-axis-sol-pi F-1.9 on a different model and harness, which strengthens the methodological conclusion: Code Mass (APP) must not be read as a quality metric in architecture comparisons.
